Strategy backtests — train (2023–25) vs. untouched test (2026H1)

Expected return per deal in basis points, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (5,000 resamples). Bars crossing zero on the CI whisker are not statistically distinguishable from noise at this sample size.

Read S1 vs S2 together: on 2026H1 both scored near-identically (+208 vs +189 bps) because the whole test period was a hot regime — the filters couldn't differentiate a uniformly good tape. The regime gate's real value is what it avoided in 2023–24, not test-period alpha. The one strategy with a genuinely live, both-periods-positive secondary edge is buy the day-1 close only after a confirmed +20% pop, sell at 20 days — but see the hardening checks below before sizing it.

S3b hardening checks — is this production-ready?

Before treating a backtested mean as a tradable edge: does it survive costs, is it concentrated in a few lucky trades, can it actually be filled at size, is it stable year to year, and what does the drawdown look like if run continuously? Each check below is a reason to size this cautiously, not a reason to discard it.

Data & methodology

Costs: subscription 1.0084% (brokerage + SFC + AFRC + trading fee) on allotted shares; sale 0.25% (stamp duty + brokerage + slippage) per side. HIBOR 1-month carry on committed capital for 6 calendar days (pre-FINI, T+5 era) or 2 days (post-FINI, T+1 era). Pool A allocation probability is the smallest-application-tier ballot rate from each deal's own Basis of Allocation announcement — multi-lot allocation curves are not modeled, so the portfolio calculator assumes the 1-lot probability applies per ballot, which overstates larger-capital outcomes (higher tiers allocate at equal-or-lower rates in every observed table).

Return corruption repair: Yahoo Finance back-adjusts its full price history after a post-listing split/bonus/consolidation. 20 of 331 companies were affected; all vs-offer returns for those companies were rescaled against Tencent's explicitly-unadjusted daily bars before any analysis in this dashboard.

Point-in-time discipline: subscription-decision strategies (S1/S2) use only information published before the subscription deadline (prospectus facts, calendar, market regime, prior-deal history). Day-1 secondary strategies (S3) additionally use the allotment results published the evening before listing. No strategy uses same-day-or-later information to make an earlier decision.
