The lead · the offshore rush, 2023–2026

The year the gong never stopped

Three and a half years, 331 listings, and a first-day median up forty-fold — yet the pop is spent before the first public trade prints.

From the desk · 331 verified filings · returns corrected to the raw tape

When the gong sounded for Fenbi Ltd. in January 2023, the median new listing closed its first day up 0.8 per cent. By the first half of 2026 the ceremony was being performed eighty-three times in six months, to a median first-day gain of 37.5 per cent. The rush is real, and accelerating.

Where the money is made is another matter. Offer to first close, the median gain is 7 per cent; opening print to close, −0.3 per cent. The pop is settled in the opening auction. Whoever holds allocation at the bell owns the day; whoever chases it buys, on average, nothing — and by one year out the median listing sits 22 per cent below its offer price.

Buy the winners, not the open

Chasing the opening print is reliably unprofitable. Buying the first close — only after a confirmed pop above twenty per cent — and holding twenty days is the one secondary trade that survived the untouched test period. Capacity is small; size accordingly.

Leave the lock-up alone

The six-month expiry, shorted into and through, returns six basis points before borrow — and borrow on a six-month-old listing in Hong Kong is scarce to prohibitive. The overhang story does not survive contact with the tape.
