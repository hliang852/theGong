    Executive summary

    The pop is real

    There were 331 Main Board listings and US$80.7bn of proceeds between January 2023 and June 2026, though most were concentrated sharply into 2025–26 (Exhibits 1–2). The mix shifted with the volume: mainland-incorporated H-shares rose from about a third of listings to six in ten, and technology roughly doubled its share (Exhibits 3–4).

    The median first day climbed from +0.8% in 2023 to +37.5% in 2026 H1 (Exhibit 5), a long right tail pulling the average above a crowded cluster near zero (Exhibits 6–7). Yet the gain is almost entirely a subscription phenomenon. Offer to first close the median is about +7%; from the opening print to the close it is −0.3% (Exhibit 8). The pop is settled in the opening auction — whoever holds allocation at the bell owns the day; whoever chases the open buys, on average: nothing.

    Demand anticipates the pop: retail oversubscription, which by 2026 routinely exceeds 1,000× (Exhibit 9), tracks the debut (Exhibits 11–13). But allocation works against the buyer — the hotter the book, the smaller the fill, and the deals actually allotted are disproportionately the ones that do not pop (Exhibits 14–15, ρ ≈ −0.35). The effect is strongest in smaller deals; the megacaps behave (Exhibit 16).

    Whatever the debut, the path fades. The median listing peaks within the first month and then declines; a year out it sits about 22% below its offer price, with only ~34% of listings above water (Exhibits 17–18). For the retail seat, primary strategy has been a 2025–26 trade, not a 2023–24 one (Exhibit 22).

    Take a look at the Simulator and Strategy pages as well on historical return on IPO participation based on various exit strategies.

    Strategy

    Three trades, taken to the tape

    Designed on 2023–25, tested once on 2026 H1. Verdicts as printed; details in Charts, exhibits 19–20.

    Conditional · live since '25

    Subscribe when the tape is up

    Balloting every deal lost money through 2023–24 and paid through the rush. One gate — subscribe only when the Hang Seng's trailing month is positive — kept the seat out of the losing years and cost nothing in the hot ones.

    Buy the winners, not the open

    Chasing the opening print is reliably unprofitable. Buying the first close — only after a confirmed pop above twenty per cent — and holding twenty days is the one secondary trade that survived the untouched test period. Capacity is small; size accordingly.

    Dead · do not run

    Leave the lock-up alone

    The six-month expiry, shorted into and through, returns six basis points before borrow — and borrow on a six-month-old listing in Hong Kong is scarce to prohibitive. The overhang story does not survive contact with the tape.

    About

    Sources, method & the desk

    The GONG is an independent record of Hong Kong Main Board listing days, read from primary filings and verified against the raw exchange tape.

    What this covers

    The GONG tracks every Main Board initial public offering on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange listed between 1 January 2023 and 30 June 2026 — 331 companies in all. It deliberately excludes cases that are not fresh capital-raising IPOs: transfers up from GEM, listings by introduction (no offer), and de-SPAC business combinations. Each company is followed from its prospectus through to its trading record.

    What we read, and where it comes from

    The primary source is the exchange’s own filing system, HKEXnews. For every deal we collect and read the full set of statutory documents:

    • Prospectus / listing document — offer structure, price range, use of proceeds, cornerstone investors, lock-ups and risk factors.
    • Allotment results announcement — the final offer price, retail and placing subscription rates, clawback, and the one-lot allocation odds.
    • Over-allotment and stabilisation notices — whether the greenshoe was exercised and how the aftermarket was supported.

    Around a thousand of these filings were retrieved directly as source PDFs and parsed; on each single-stock view we link straight back to the original prospectus and allotment result on HKEXnews so any figure can be checked against the primary document.

    How the returns are measured

    Prices and volumes are taken from daily exchange bars, not from a retail data feed. This matters: retail feeds routinely back-adjust a company’s entire price history after a split or bonus issue, which silently corrupts every return computed against the original offer price. We cross-checked all 331 series against unadjusted exchange bars and repaired the 20 names where the feed had restated history, so a “+70%” debut that was really +8% reads correctly.

    First-day figures are split into offer→open, open→close and offer→close so the debut can be separated from the auction. Longer horizons (5-day, 1-month, 3-month, and the 6-month and 1-year volume-weighted average price) are computed the same way, weighting each day’s typical price by that day’s volume. Where a horizon has not yet elapsed for a recent listing, the figure is shown as unavailable rather than filled with a placeholder.

    How the strategies are tested

    Every trading rule is designed on 2023–2025 data and then run once, untouched, on the first half of 2026 — a genuine out-of-sample test rather than a curve fit. All results are net of realistic frictions: the 1.01% subscription cost, 0.25% per side on secondary sales, and HIBOR financing carry on capital locked up during the subscription period. Expected values are weighted by each deal’s actual allocation odds, because winning the ballot on a hot deal is the exception, not the rule.
