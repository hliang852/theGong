The lead · the offshore rush, 2023–2026

The year the gong never stopped

Three and a half years, 331 Main Board listings, and a first-day median that rose forty-fold — yet the whole of the average pop is spent before the first trade prints. A study of who the rush actually paid.

From the desk · compiled from 331 verified filings and the unadjusted tape

On the ninth of January, 2023, the gong at Exchange Square sounded for Fenbi Ltd., and the market shrugged: the median new listing that year closed its first day up 0.8 per cent. By the first half of 2026 the same ceremony was being performed eighty-three times in six months, to a median first-day gain of 37.5 per cent, with four of every five debuts finishing above their offer price. The rush — mainland issuers seeking offshore listings through Hong Kong — is real, and it is accelerating.

What the ceremony conceals is where the money is made. Across all 331 listings, the median gain from offer to first close is 7 per cent; the median gain from the opening print to the close is −0.3 per cent. The pop exists, and it is settled in the opening auction, before any public buyer has traded. Whoever holds allocation at 9:30 owns the day; whoever chases at the bell, on average, buys nothing.

The subscription seat is therefore the story. Adjusted for ballot odds, fees and financing, a one-lot Pool A subscriber earned an expected +46 basis points per deal at the first close — a real edge, a small one, and a recent one: the same strategy lost money in 2023 and 2024. Allocation odds and returns run opposite each other (−0.35): the deals you are given are the deals nobody wanted.

And the fade is unsentimental. By six months the median listing sits below its offer; by a year, 22 per cent below, with a third still above water. The gong marks a beginning that is, for the median holder, also the high.

Page 3 · The Anatomy

Pop, then fade

The median listing's life against its offer price: +7 per cent on day one, drifting to +11 per cent at one month — then the turn. Six months out the median deal is under water; at a year it is 22 per cent below offer, and only a third of the class of 2023–25 still trades above its issue price.

The mean tells a friendlier story, but only because a handful of listings run to multiples. The typical deal — the one you are most likely to hold — peaks within weeks of the gong. Whatever the strategy, the data argues for an early exit convention, not conviction holding.

Subscribe when the tape is up

Balloting every deal lost money through 2023–24 and paid through the rush. A single gate — subscribe only when the Hang Seng's trailing month is positive — kept the seat out of the losing years and cost nothing in the hot ones.

Buy the winners, not the open

Chasing the opening print is reliably unprofitable. Buying the first close — only after a confirmed pop above twenty per cent — and holding twenty days is the one secondary trade that survived an untouched test period. Capacity is small; size accordingly.

Leave the lock-up alone

The six-month expiry, shorted into and through, returns six basis points before borrow — and borrow on a six-month-old listing in Hong Kong is scarce to prohibitive. The overhang story does not survive contact with the tape.
